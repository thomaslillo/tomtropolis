Vibe coding makes it really hard to think because you can just hit the easy button. When an AI assistant can produce an answer, write the code, or explain an error immediately, it becomes very tempting to skip the uncomfortable part where you sit with the problem and work out what you think.

Metacognitive awareness is being aware of your own thinking and being able to recognize the traps you fall into. More importantly, it means being able to work your way out of those traps. The goal is not to avoid every mistake or difficulty. It is to notice what is happening while you are in the middle of it.

The paper [*The Widening Gap: The Benefits and Harms of Generative AI for Novice Programmers*](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17739v1) discusses this in more detail and shows how metacognitive difficulties appear in new programmers. Some students were able to use generative AI to move faster because they already had an idea of what they wanted to do and could ignore suggestions that were wrong or unhelpful. For students who were struggling, though, the same tools could compound existing difficulties and create new ones.

AI can make these traps even worse, and this is not just a problem for new programmers. I have found that it can happen anytime you do a new thing in an area of programming that you were previously unfamiliar with. Even if you are an experienced programmer, unfamiliar territory can put you in the position of a beginner. You may not yet have enough context to recognize whether you are making progress, whether an answer is correct, or whether the tool has quietly taken over the problem-solving process.

## What this means in practice

The following is Table 2, “Definitions of Old and New Metacognitive Difficulties,” from *The Widening Gap*. It is a useful way to name the traps that can show up while learning or working with AI-assisted programming ([Prather et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17739v1)).

| Difficulty | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Forming** | Forming an incorrect approach or conceptual model of the problem. |
| **Dislodging** | Struggling to abandon an approach after realizing that it is not working. |
| **Assumption** | Making an untested assumption about what the problem requires and solving the wrong problem. |
| **Location** | Missing an important part of the problem-solving process and not realizing where the gap is. |
| **Achievement** | Continuing to make small fixes to a fundamentally broken approach instead of recognizing that it needs to be reconsidered. |
| **Progression** | Falling behind in your understanding without noticing because AI can produce work that appears to show progress. |
| **Interruption** | Losing your train of thought when AI suggestions interrupt the process of working through a problem. |
| **Mislead** | Following an AI suggestion that seems plausible but takes the solution in the wrong direction. |

These difficulties are easier to see from the outside than they are to notice in yourself. That is why naming them matters. When the easy button is available, metacognitive awareness gives you a reason to pause and ask whether you are actually learning, understanding, and solving the problem—or just producing something that looks finished.
